# Reader invitations: confident, curious, occasional

Draft copy for selection. No website rollout or email sending is included.

## Voice and behavior

A specific risk, a little wit, and a credible next step. The joke should make the invitation easier to accept; the last thought should be evidence, a useful safeguard, or a path to a fix. Avoid tired-workshop humor, grandiose safety promises, diagnosing the reader, invented case studies, or implying a predictable email schedule.

Every invitation offers three legitimate choices: get occasional updates, read something now without submitting an email, or dismiss and continue. The extra reading link is intentional. It should open separately and preserve the current article and any entered email. Never withhold the promised reading behind signup.

**Shared email expectation:** “A newsletter is on our list. For now, only important updates—perhaps a couple a year. Unsubscribe anytime.”

**Default action:** “Keep me in the loop.”

**Default skip:** “Just here to read.”

## 1. The refund: practical risk, light delivery

**Headline:** Your refund bot is feeling generous.

**Copy:** Imagine a $50 refund becoming $550. Bug, abuse, or a rule nobody enforced? We trace what happened, close the gap, and test the fix.

**Side note:** We’re here in the margin. Your article is still yours.

**Read first:** [Read how we test security](https://www.17thstreetlabs.com/blog/continuous-security-testing/)

**Placement:** Security and agent-oversight articles. The money example is explicitly hypothetical, not a reported customer incident.

## 2. The screenshot: a verified curiosity hook

**Headline:** A $1 car. An expensive screenshot.

**Copy:** A dealership chatbot agreed to a $1 Tahoe. That was a chatbot response, not evidence of a completed sale. The useful question: what can your agent actually authorize? We test the boundaries and keep the evidence.

**Read first:** [The dealership story — Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/car-dealership-chevrolet-chatbot-chatgpt-pranks-chevy-2023-12)

**Placement:** Agent permissions and business rules. “Expensive” is a figurative reputational hook, not a quantified loss claim. December 2023 reporting, not current breaking news.

## 3. The proof: replace the exhausted-writer joke

**Headline:** Fixed is good. Proven is better.

**Copy:** We find the failure, make it reproducible, and test the fix. Then we keep the test, so the same surprise has a harder time coming back.

**Close:** Get the important findings when we have them. No calendar-filling dispatches.

**Read first:** [Read how we evaluate agents](https://www.17thstreetlabs.com/blog/eval-driven-agent-development/)

**Placement:** Testing and evaluation articles. The animated note changes “It seems fine.” into “Here’s the evidence.” It edits the note, never deletes actual article content.

## 4. The intent: do not confuse an outcome with a motive

**Headline:** Helpful customer. Creative accountant. Same chat box.

**Copy:** You can’t infer intent from a refund request. You can record what the agent was asked, what it did, and which rule it crossed. We build checks around that evidence.

**Close:** Fewer guesses. A clearer next step.

**Read first:** [What is your agent actually doing?](https://www.17thstreetlabs.com/blog/security-copilot-that-explains/)

**Placement:** Oversight, logs, and incident review. Do not label a user an attacker just because an outcome is wrong.

## 5. The shopkeeper: the joke belongs to the business rule

**Headline:** Charming shopkeeper. Unfortunate margins.

**Copy:** Anthropic’s experimental AI shopkeeper could be talked into discounts and gave some items away. A friendly answer and a sound business decision are different tests. We check both.

**Read first:** [Read Anthropic’s experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1)

**Placement:** Agent economics or permissions. Clearly call it an experiment, not a retail deployment or a claim about the current model.

## 6. The quiet failure: evidence before applause

**Headline:** “No complaints” is a lovely metric. Until it isn’t.

**Copy:** Did the task finish correctly, or did the user just leave? We check the steps and the result, so silence doesn’t get mistaken for success.

**Read first:** [What a browser recording can reveal](https://www.17thstreetlabs.com/blog/agents-that-see-the-ui/)

**Placement:** Interface testing. A question to investigate, not a claim about a particular customer's churn.

## 7. The periphery: a restrained evergreen alternative

**Headline:** Small note. Actual point.

**Copy:** Your AI can sound certain and still cross a boundary. We test what it can do, what it should refuse, and how to tell the difference. You can read about that without giving us anything.

**Read first:** [Start with the security checks](https://www.17thstreetlabs.com/blog/continuous-security-testing/)

**Placement:** The side note, anywhere the security topic is relevant. Straightens on pointer or keyboard engagement; no pulsing, repeated wobble, scroll lock, or countdown.

## 8. The calendar: seasonal, never the default

**Headline:** The newsletter has made the list. Again.

**Copy:** Our New Year’s list includes starting a newsletter. Before we negotiate with the calendar, we’re sticking to important updates about AI failures, fixes, and the evidence that matters. Perhaps a couple a year.

**Read first:** [Browse the Blog](https://www.17thstreetlabs.com/blog/)

**Placement:** Late December through early January only, reviewed before reuse. Not active in the September mockups. No gym joke, abandoned-resolution guilt, or promise of monthly delivery.

## Evidence and editorial limits

- [Business Insider, December 18, 2023](https://www.businessinsider.com/car-dealership-chevrolet-chatbot-chatgpt-pranks-chevy-2023-12) supports the dealership-chatbot example. Avoid claiming the vehicle was sold or the offer was legally binding.
- [Anthropic, June 27, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1) directly documents its shop experiment's discounts, free items, and below-cost sales. [Phase two, December 18, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2) describes improvements alongside continued vulnerabilities. Treat these as dated experiments, not universal model-performance claims.
- The recollection of a lifetime supply of Cheetos or another food product negotiated down to pennies was not verified. It is excluded from factual copy.
- The proposed future article angles are refund authorization versus persuasion, attempted abuse versus observed harm, and regression checks for business rules. These are ideas, not new articles claimed to be written or published.
- Copy cannot promise that all attacks will be prevented or all malicious intent identified. The concrete offer is reproducible testing, explicit controls, and inspectable evidence.
