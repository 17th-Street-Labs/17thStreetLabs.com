# 3. The Sharpest Brand Truth

## A 10% fail rate is a 90% pass rate.

Sounds good.

Until the 10% is the part your customer remembers.

A system that succeeds 90% of the time can still be completely unusable.

A support agent that gives harmful advice 1 out of 10 times is not “90% accurate.”

A workflow that silently fails every tenth run is not “mostly reliable.”

An AI that does the right thing nine times and something inexplicable on the tenth is not production-ready just because the dashboard is green.

### Variants

**10% fail = 90% pass.  
And 90% pass can still be a disaster.**

**Your AI passes 90% of the time.  
Cool. What happens in the other 10%?**

**90% success sounds impressive until failure matters.**

**If your AI fails 10% of the time, you don’t have a 90% success problem.  
You have a failure problem.**

**“It works 90% of the time” is not the flex people think it is.**

**A green dashboard can hide a very expensive 10%.**

AI changes the nature of software.

You used to write code and know, more or less, what it would do.

Now you can build systems that reason, improvise, call tools, delegate work, use memory, interpret images, navigate software, and take action.

- agents
- harnesses
- orchestration
- workflows
- regression tests
- adversarial tests
- model routing
- observability

So teams can know what changed, what improved, what broke, and why.

# 9. Tone

## Smart without performing smart

Use technical language when the technical language is the most precise language.

Then translate it.

Do not dilute expertise.

Do not make the reader work unnecessarily hard either.

---

## Confident without corporate chest-beating

The confidence should come from specifics.

Better:

> We test trajectories, regressions, judge variance, adversarial behavior, and the failure modes your happy-path demo never sees.

Worse:

> We are world-class AI experts delivering best-in-class solutions.

Humor should make the engineering truth more memorable.

Examples:

> Give an intelligent system the wrong architecture and congratulations, you’ve invented an extremely expensive intern with root access.

> Your eval says everything is fine. Your users disagree.

> Somehow you’re spending HOW much on tokens?

> A green dashboard can hide a very expensive 10%.

---

## Slightly irreverent

We should sound like people who build this stuff, break this stuff, and have opinions about it.

Not like a committee approved every sentence.

# 18. Website Section: Model Strategy

## Bigger is not automatically smarter.

The AI industry has developed an expensive habit:

**Throw the biggest model at it.**

Sometimes that is exactly the right answer.

Sometimes it is wildly unnecessary.

A frontier model may be right for one part of the system.

A small local model may be better for another.

A specialized model may outperform both.

One expensive agent may lose to 100 cheap agents running in parallel.

The point is not to pick a favorite model.

The point is to build the right system.

### Measure

- capability
- cost
- latency
- privacy
- reliability
- throughput
- context requirements
- operational complexity

### Core line

**The model is a component. The system is the product.**

# 19. Website Section: AI Economics

## Every token has a price.

The current AI economy makes it easy to forget that.

Subsidized subscriptions create the impression that unlimited intelligence is cheap.

Production systems tell a different story.

Every:

- tool call
- retry
- agent loop
- evaluation
- background task
- long context
- model escalation

AI-native engineering includes understanding the economics of the intelligence you are using.

### Possible line

**The smartest model in the world is not very smart if the unit economics do not work.**
